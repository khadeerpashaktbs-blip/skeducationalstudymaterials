Indian Polity & Governance 01 · PRINT-READY NOTES

How to use this chapter: Read the concept explanations first, revise the article map, then attempt the quick recall points. Use the blue Download PDF button to save or print this complete chapter.

1. The Constitution at a glance

The Constitution is the supreme law of India. It establishes the organs of government, distributes powers between the Union and the States, protects individual rights and provides mechanisms for responsible, limited government. It was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950.

Drafting bodyConstituent Assembly
Chairman, Drafting CommitteeDr. B. R. Ambedkar
Constitution Day26 November

2. The Preamble

The Preamble expresses the constitutional identity and objectives of the Republic. Sovereign means India is internally supreme and externally independent. Socialist indicates a commitment to social and economic justice. Secular means the State has no official religion and maintains principled equality among faiths. Democratic means authority flows from the people. Republic means the head of State is elected, directly or indirectly.

The words “Socialist”, “Secular” and “Integrity” were added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976. The Supreme Court has held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution and helps interpret its provisions, although it is not an independent source of power.

3. Fundamental Rights: Articles 12–35

Fundamental Rights protect civil liberties and limit arbitrary State action. They are justiciable, meaning a person can approach the courts when a protected right is violated. They are not absolute: reasonable restrictions may apply for public order, security, morality and other constitutionally permitted grounds.

RightArticlesWhat to remember
Equality14–18Equality before law, non-discrimination, equal opportunity, abolition of untouchability and titles.
Freedom19–22Speech, assembly, association, movement, residence and profession; protection in criminal conviction and life/personal liberty.
Against exploitation23–24Prohibits trafficking, forced labour and hazardous employment of children.
Freedom of religion25–28Conscience, practice and propagation subject to public order, morality and health.
Cultural & educational rights29–30Protects language, script, culture and minority educational institutions.
Constitutional remedies32Right to approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

4. Constitutional writs

  • Habeas Corpus: “Produce the body”; protects against unlawful detention.
  • Mandamus: “We command”; directs a public authority to perform a legal duty.
  • Prohibition: Stops a lower court or tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction.
  • Certiorari: Quashes an order of a lower court or tribunal for jurisdictional error.
  • Quo Warranto: Questions the legal authority by which a person holds a public office.

5. Union executive and judiciary

The President is the constitutional head of the Union (Article 52), while executive action is exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister (Articles 74–75). The Council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. The Supreme Court, established under Article 124, has original jurisdiction in Union–State disputes, appellate jurisdiction and advisory jurisdiction under Article 143.

Exam recall: Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right. Article 226 gives High Courts writ power for Fundamental Rights and “for any other purpose”, making its scope wider than Article 32.
UPSC · History

History & Freedom Struggle 02

  • Ancient dynasties: Indus Valley urban planning, Ashoka’s Dhamma under the Mauryas and the Gupta Golden Age.
  • Key milestones: 1857 First War of Independence; 1885 INC formation; 1905 Swadeshi Movement.
  • Gandhian era: Non-Cooperation (1920), Dandi March (1930) and Quit India Movement (1942).
KPSC · Karnataka

Karnataka History & Heritage 03

  • Kadambas: Founded by Mayurasharma (345 AD); Halmidi inscription is the earliest stone record in Kannada.
  • Chalukyas: Pulakeshin II defeated Harshavardhana on the banks of the Narmada.
  • Unification: Led by Aluru Venkata Rao. Mysore State formed on November 1, 1956, and renamed Karnataka in 1973.
KPSC · Kannada

General Kannada Literature 04

  • Sandhi: Lopa, Agama and Adesha in Kannada; Savarnadeergha, Guna, Vriddhi and Yan in Sanskrit.
  • Samasa: Tatpurusha, Karmadharaya, Dvandva, Dvigu and Bahuvrihi.
  • Jnanpith: Kannada recipients include Kuvempu, Bendre, Karanth, Masti, Gokak, Ananthamurthy, Karnad and Kambara.
KEA · Aptitude

Quantitative Aptitude 05

  • Speed: Speed = Distance ÷ Time. Convert km/h to m/s by multiplying by 5/18.
  • Work & Time: If A finishes in X days and B in Y, combined time = XY ÷ (X + Y).
  • Profit: Profit % = (Profit ÷ Cost Price) × 100.
KEA · Karnataka

Government Flagship Schemes 06

  • Gruha Lakshmi: ₹2,000 monthly support for female heads of family.
  • Gruha Jyothi: Up to 200 units of free electricity for households.
  • Yuva Nidhi & Shakti: Stipends for unemployed graduates and free public bus travel for women.
